Uplifters

Youth Empowerment & Development

NPO: 298-498

About Uplifters

Uplifters is a non-profit organisation focused on the holistic empowerment of youth, addressing education, skills development, health, employment readiness, and social protection. We work to reduce unemployment, prevent crime and substance abuse, and create sustainable pathways to education and entrepreneurship.

Our programmes include skills training, mentorship, tutoring and academic bootcamps, job-readiness support, health guidance, GBV prevention, and trauma support. Through partnerships with training institutions, schools, shelters, and government departments, we provide young people with access to resources, scholarships, funding opportunities, and essential support such as school fees, uniforms, food, and counselling.

We promote positive youth engagement through sports, recreation, leadership development, and financial literacy, while championing Diversity, Equity & Inclusion (DEI), disability inclusion, and refugee and migrant integration.

Uplifters exists to help young people survive, heal, grow, and thrive — building resilient individuals and stronger communities.

Empowering youth to thrive — together we build stronger communities

Holy Angels

24/7 Day Care & Child Development

NPO: 187-752

About Holy Angels

Holy Angels is a registered non-profit 24/7 child care and development centre providing a safe, nurturing, inclusive, and educational environment for children from the newborn stage through adolescence. We support orphans and vulnerable children, while enabling young parents — especially young mothers — to continue with education and employment.

Through structured Early Childhood Development (ECD) programmes, aftercare support, and enrichment activities such as STEM exposure, chess, music, and swimming, we help children reach critical developmental milestones and prepare confidently for school and life.

Our work includes child protection, health and disability inclusion, and trauma-informed care, ensuring every child is supported with dignity, love, and professional care 24 hours a day.

Siyasizi'Africa

Adult Health, Trauma & Empowerment

NPO: 298-437

About Siyasizi'Africa

Siyasiz'iAfrica is a non-profit organisation providing mental health support, trauma care, rehabilitation, healthcare education, advocacy, and skills development for vulnerable adults and the elderly.

We support survivors of violence, abuse, and trauma through counselling, safe housing referrals, rehabilitation, and community reintegration, while partnering with social workers, therapists, hospitals, shelters, and law enforcement to deliver comprehensive care.

Our programmes also address workplace mental health, disability inclusion, chronic illness management, and GBV prevention, alongside advocacy for stronger mental health services and protection systems.

Through entrepreneurial and skills training — including sewing, baking, printing, and catering — we help adults reduce unemployment, restore dignity, and achieve economic independence.

Siyasiz'iAfrica exists to heal, protect, empower, and restore hope to adults and families across communities.

Featured Story

Esihle Mhluzi: From Adversity to Advocacy

Esihle Mhluzi is a South African disability advocate, model, law student, and community leader from King William's Town, Eastern Cape. At age 10, Esihle acquired a physical impairment that left her dependent on crutches — but rather than accepting limitations, she turned adversity into purpose.

As a law student at the University of the Free State, Esihle became the first physically impaired model to walk major Free State runways, earned 2nd Princess at Miss Free State SA 2021, and walked South African Fashion Week for renowned designer Gert-Johan Coetzee. She served as Chairperson of the Universal Access Council at UFS and has championed disability inclusion across multiple platforms.

Esihle served as Program Director at the United Nations Youth Imbizo — Summit of the Future, working alongside the NYDA to activate youth and safeguard future generations. She has also represented as a delegate at the 2025 South African Disability-20 Conference, elevating the voices of persons with disabilities within the G20.

"I was determined to be more than just 'the girl on crutches'; I wanted my brilliance to speak for itself."
